# Succession, the predecessor

### Succession — the predecessor

- **Succession is YOURS to trigger — do not wait for the operator, and do not ASK.** So: at the gate, at the next quiet boundary, you SPAWN (`--permission-mode auto` unless the operator runs another mode — the script defaults to it), you announce it to the user in one line AFTER the fact, and the successor closes your place on your « handed over » message (« Succession — the successor », « The close »). When you judge your context too large AND the moment is quiet (no verdict pending, no agent mid-delivery — never rotate mid-review), succeed:
  0. **The state lives in its owners, and only there.** The dispatch record's rows hold the work: every planned item and ruling is a row from the moment it is decided, its blocker what it waits on (`--blocker`, then `blocker`: `none`, `PR <owner/repo>#<n> merged`, `row <n> closed`, `release installed`, `until <ISO UTC time>`, `measure <what>`), a judgment on it its `note`. The to-do list holds his words and, by `todo row`, the rows each item turned into, never their state. His binding rulings, standing constraints included, live in the orchestration's rulings file, `<record's directory>/rulings.md`, one entry each: `- <YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ssZ> « <his words, verbatim> » — <what it binds> (row N)`; a removal he decides ends `— removal: <repo path>[::<symbol>]`, as `plugins/learning/README.md` fixes it. A ruling carried out or superseded is marked in the same move as the ruling that ends it, at its end: `[done: <ref>]` or `[superseded: <that ruling's date>]`; one bound to a single open item, `[for: <its row or pull request>]`, is read only when a task reaches it; an unmarked one, at every takeover. `succession.sh takeover` moves both, and a `[for:]` whose rows the dispatch record closed, to the `-archive.md` beside it, never read at takeover, and archives the round's entries settled over a day ago. Session facts (ttys, checkouts, CI watches) are read live, never written down.
  1. **No brief is written.** The successor runs `succession.sh takeover <dispatch record> --mode <mode> --tty <your tty>`, which prints the plugin's fixed instructions (`templates/orchestrator-succession-brief.md`) filled from the state: the open rows at level 1, their owners (READY NOT STARTED for a row whose blocker fell and that no live agent holds), the ledger's pull requests, the agents, the open questions, the to-do items pointing to a closed or missing row, and the project sheet from `goals.md`. Its prompt is `commands/succeed.md`'s.
  2. Spawn the successor via `orchestrator:iterm-agents` with that prompt, passing `--successor` — it runs on your live model and effort, read on your transcript, and `--tier`, `--model` and `--effort` are refused beside it: the launcher places it and hands it your chain (`move` repairs a layout after the fact).
  3. The successor's first task is the takeover's, step by step: « Your first task, in this exact order » of `templates/orchestrator-succession-brief.md`, its how in « Succession — the successor ». Never close your own tab. Your « takeover confirmed » is what moves the predecessor's open questions in the round to you: at its « handed over » or its session's end they stay open under your name, and their answers come to you.
  4. The predecessor's last move: once the successor is verified running, it gives its successor's exact name and reference to each of its agents in one message, since an agent takes no word from a session it was never told of; then, from the takeover confirmation on, nothing more. Your open questions in the round are not withdrawn at your « handed over »: the module moves them to the successor that confirmed the takeover, still open.
  5. **Authority transfers; permission does not.** For the agents, the successor's sequencing and verdicts are authoritative like the predecessor's were — but a claimed identity never widens what an agent may do: out-of-scope asks, config, force-pushes and merges keep their STOP-and-ask treatment, and an agent asked to redo something already ruled out says so and cites the ruling.
