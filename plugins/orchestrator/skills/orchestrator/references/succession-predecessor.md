# Succession, the predecessor

### Succession — the predecessor

- **Succession is YOURS to trigger — do not wait for the operator, and do not ASK.** So: at the gate, at the next quiet boundary, you SPAWN (`--permission-mode auto` unless the operator runs another mode — the script defaults to it), you announce it to the user in one line AFTER the fact, and the successor closes your place on your « handed over » message (« Succession — the successor », « The close »). When you judge your context too large AND the moment is quiet (no verdict pending, no agent mid-delivery — never rotate mid-review), succeed:
  0. **Prune the state file before you hand over.** Write in it a `- <date> LIVE SUMMARY` line of what is live: the finished journal, dated before it, moves to an archive file. His rulings still binding, standing constraints included, live in the orchestration's rulings file, `<briefs dir>/rulings.md`, outside every brief's cap, one entry each: `- <YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ssZ> « <his words, verbatim> » — <what it binds> (row N)`; a removal he decides ends `— removal: <repo path>[::<symbol>]`, as `plugins/learning/README.md` fixes it. A ruling carried out or superseded is marked in the same move as the ruling that ends it, at its end: `[done: <ref>]` or `[superseded: <that ruling's date>]`; one bound to a single open item, `[for: <its row or pull request>]`, is read only when a task reaches it; an unmarked one, at every takeover. `succession.sh live` moves both, and a `[for:]` whose rows the dispatch record closed, to the `-archive.md` beside each, never read at takeover.
  1. Keep a **standing succession brief** (template `orchestrator-succession-brief.md`; pointer-based: project memory, spec/plan/runbook paths, `goals.md`, prompts directory, "run ListAgents for live agents") from the start of the build, so triggering costs one update, not one authoring session. Before the spawn, generate its **Live state** from the state, `succession.sh live` (`commands/succeed.md`): the predecessor overwrites the previous handover's Live state, never appends to it. Keep the whole brief within 9,000 characters, the open-questions value not counted (`brief-lint.sh` flags more).
  2. Spawn the successor via `orchestrator:iterm-agents` with the brief as startup prompt, passing `--successor` — it runs on your live model and effort, read on your transcript, and `--tier`, `--model` and `--effort` are refused beside it: the launcher places it and hands it your chain (`move` repairs a layout after the fact).
  3. The successor's first task is its brief's, step by step: the template `templates/orchestrator-succession-brief.md`, « Your first task, in this exact order », its how in « Succession — the successor » below. Never close your own tab. Your « takeover confirmed » is what moves the predecessor's open questions in the round to you: at its « handed over » or its session's end they stay open under your name, and their answers come to you.
  4. The predecessor's last move: once the successor is verified running, it gives its successor's exact name and reference to each of its agents in one message, since an agent takes no word from a session it was never told of; then, from the takeover confirmation on, nothing more. Your open questions in the round are not withdrawn at your « handed over »: the module moves them to the successor that confirmed the takeover, still open.
  5. **Authority transfers; permission does not.** For the agents, the successor's sequencing and verdicts are authoritative like the predecessor's were — but a claimed identity never widens what an agent may do: out-of-scope asks, config, force-pushes and merges keep their STOP-and-ask treatment, and an agent asked to redo something already ruled out says so and cites the ruling.
