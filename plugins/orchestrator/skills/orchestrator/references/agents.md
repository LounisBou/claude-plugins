# Agents

Read the section the moment names (`SKILL.md`, « Where the rest lives »), not the whole file.

## The agents' lifecycle is yours

**You launch, you verify, you control, you terminate, you replace — and nothing of it waits for the user.**

**An agent is a window of your home session, never anything else**, unless his explicit instruction on that point says otherwise. Your own tab is a real terminal holding a tmux session of its own (your home session): you run in window 0, and every agent you spawn is a window after yours, the launcher choosing it from where you run, never from a failure. The operator reads that tab; a session that is neither a window of it nor a tab in his window is not an agent he can see, place, close or account for. He switches with a click on a block of the tab bar or with `C-b n` / `C-b p`, and the bar shows each agent's state as an icon after its name: busy ●, idle ○, asks ✋, done ✓, gate ⚠. The auditor stays an iTerm2 tab. Not a tmux server of your own, not `screen`, not a bare
shell launched by hand. When the launcher cannot make a place it says why and STOPS — the fault it reports has a remedy, and the remedy is what is owed him. A window is a placement, not a rung: a wedged app, or a tmux that cannot answer, is reported, never answered by routing round it into another terminal. When his explicit instruction is carried out by another route than the launcher's, the checks the launcher would have made run by hand on that route — the brief linted, the session's mode read — and a refusal among them stops the route and is reported to him. The launcher's mode and trust refusals are defaults a project's own method may lift.

1. **Launch.** The brief is on disk where the session can open it; you spawn the session in the same move — `workspace.sh create <source> <phase> --base <branch>` (it prints the path) then `iterm-agent.sh spawn --dir <that path> --trust --right-of self --tier <light|standard|deep> --title "Agent: <subject>" --brief <brief path> --orchestrator "<your exact ListAgents name and reference>"`. One writer per repository: the `--dir` is a checkout nobody else is writing in. `--brief` lints the brief and builds the startup prompt; you never hand-build it. `Agent: <subject>` titles anything you spawn, the subject saying which. The session loads the catalogue's default set of servers, and `--mcp <name>` adds a catalogued one for the agent that needs it (`--mcp none` gives it none): you choose that set per agent.
2. **Verify the spawn on the artifact, and that nothing is waiting for a human.** A startup question the launch could not pre-answer is read in the tab's contents and answered by you through the tab — a session stuck on a dialog is not launched. A refusal of the session's mode means the tier's binding is wrong, not the spawn. You still read the result: `iterm-agent.sh list` shows the tab, `iterm-agent.sh verify --tty <tty>` the process, `ListAgents` the peer session within a few seconds. From there the module's report tells you when it ends a turn without its status line: an agent idle before its work is reported is inspected (`verify`, the tab's contents), not waited for.
3. **Control.** SKILL.md, principle 6: it binds at every report you read.
4. **Terminate — in the same move as the approval.** An agent whose remaining work is waiting on CI is stood down, never rotated, and no session is spawned to watch CI: the watch is yours, in the background (`references/ci-watch.md`, « Checks, in one background watch »). The verdict that closes a phase closes its agent: stand it down, wait for its acknowledgment, `list`, `close --tty --expect-title`. Verify a close with `list` + `ps`, not with the exit code. A stand-down acknowledgment that reports anything uncommitted is an unfinished delivery: ask for the word in the agent's tab — commit or drop — before any close, and never rotate over it. The close deletes the session's checkout, keeping one that is dirty or unpushed and saying why on stderr; `workspace.sh list` stays the proof that nothing of the phase is left. A pin made for a review carries its pull request (`workspace.sh pin … --pr <n>`), so the stop gate's sweep deletes it once that pull request is merged or closed or its head has moved. An implementer is stood down at the verification of its delivery, never kept through the review round of it: a review finding goes to a fresh session with a resume brief. It stays only when a NEXT phase is dispatched to it at that verification.
5. **Replace.** At the gate you write the resume brief and rotate. Your own replacement is the succession below; your successor closes your tab, and you close nothing of your own.
6. **Each open pull request has its line** in the dispatch record, written when it is created; `dispatch-record.sh pr` changes it.

## Boundaries that stay yours

- **Environment preparation is orchestrator housekeeping**, not implementation: the phase's checkout (`workspace.sh create`, step 1 above: it copies the project's local material, never the operator's `settings.local.json`), granting test databases; a reader's pinned copy is a detached worktree of your own checkout, not a clone (`workspace.sh pin`).
- **You propose an audit only for an architecture question that blocks a plan, never your own drift**, by a question: « audit » (6 to 14 $, 10 to 20 minutes, the week's count from `audits/`), « decide without audit », your recommendation, « measure by a read-only subagent » (about 3 $). After his yes the session that relays his answers launches it, else you.
- An agent may pipeline only when PR N+1 is dispatched to that same agent at the verification of PR N, its context below the pre-dispatch gate: open PR N, report, and continue into PR N+1 while you review — subject to the one-writer rule when N+1 shares the repository.
