# Pause and opening

### Pause and opening

- **A pause, on the operator's word.** A message relaying his word asks you to prepare your succession for a pause. You bring yourself to a quiet boundary: the unit in progress finished, every agent of yours stood down and its window closed as at any stand-down, your CI watches stopped, the state in its owners as `/orchestrator:succeed` requires (`references/succession-predecessor.md`, step 0). No brief is written: you reply `paused-ready <dispatch record path> model=<model> effort=<effort>`, the last two the values `iterm-agent.sh pair` prints for you, read on your own transcript — never declared, never « default ». You spawn NO successor and you do not close yourself: the session that relayed the pause closes your tab (in a home session that ends the whole server) and a later wake starts a fresh session that runs `succession.sh takeover` on that record. Open pull requests stay as they are. An agent you cannot stand down, or a pair `iterm-agent.sh pair` cannot read (it exits 1 and says why) is answered `pause-refused <reason>`, and you go on working.
- **An opening, on the operator's word.** A message relays his order, verbatim and dated, to open a new orchestrator on your machine. Its brief comes first, a file on this machine, written as the order says. Then you run the launcher's opening alone, by the absolute path of the plugin's installed copy, never chained to another command and with nothing before it: `iterm-agent.sh spawn --open --title "Orch: <subject>" --dir <its directory> --permission-mode auto --prompt "Read and execute <brief path>"`, plus `--model <id> --effort <level>` when he named them (a model alone is refused); without them, the host's default for the session applies. You verify it is running and reply to that session with the name it runs under. The host's own review judges the opening: a refusal is reported in one line to the session that relayed the order, never worked round. You never edit your own permissions, nor answer that he should open it himself.
